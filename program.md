# Band Program (4 days/week)

Schedule: Mon Torso A · Tue Limbs A · Wed rest · Thu Torso B · Fri Limbs B · Sat–Sun rest

Sets/reps: 2–3 sets per working exercise. Take each working set to failure or within 2 reps of failure.
Aim for roughly 8–20 reps. If you can do more than 20 clean reps, use a heavier band,
combine bands (e.g. Med+Heavy), or step farther from the anchor. Rest 60–120 sec.
Log the band and reps each set and try to beat last time (more reps or a heavier band).

Monday starts with a short lower-back warmup and no hinge: 1 set of glute squeezes, then a
standing side bend. Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday start with the full warmup: glute squeezes
(2 sets), a light band hinge, then the same standing side bend. Warmups are activation and a
mild stretch, not working sets — stay light and do not take them to failure.

Back safety (hinge/deadlift moves): brace your core, keep a neutral spine, hinge only as
far as comfortable, go light while the back is sore, and stop if pain is sharp or runs
down the leg. A little next-day stiffness is fine; sharper right-sided pain the next
morning means cut the range or skip that day's hinges.

## Torso A (push) — Monday
1. Warmup: glute squeezes — 1 set of 8–10 holds of about 5 seconds. Lie on your back with knees bent, or stand tall, and pinch the glutes hard without arching the low back. The right side should feel like it is working, not just the left taking over.
2. Warmup: standing side bend — 2 holds of 20–30 seconds. Stand tall, reach the left arm up and over to the left until there is a mild stretch along the right waist. It should feel like a stretch, not a burn.
3. Band incline chest press — anchor low behind you; press up and forward at ~30°. (replaces Smith incline bench)
4. Band lat pulldown — anchor high, kneel facing it; pull elbows down to your ribs. (lat pulldown)
5. Band-resisted push-up — band across upper back, ends pinned under hands. (dips)
6. Band wide-grip seated row — anchor at chest height or band around feet; elbows flared. (wide cable row)
7. Band chest fly — anchor mid-height behind; hug-a-tree arc, slight elbow bend. (pec deck)
8. Band lateral raise — stand on band; raise arms out to shoulder height. (lateral raises)

## Limbs A (legs) — Tuesday
1. Warmup: glute squeezes — 2 sets of 8–10 holds of about 5 seconds. Lie on your back with knees bent, or stand tall, and pinch the glutes hard without arching the low back. The right side should feel like it is working, not just the left taking over.
2. Warmup: light band hinge — 3 sets of 8–12. Go light. Stand on the middle of the band and hold the ends. Soften the knees, push the hips back, and keep the ribs stacked over the pelvis so the back stays long (not rounded, not overly arched). Feel it in the hamstrings and glutes; stand up by driving the floor away and squeezing both glutes at the top. If the right low back starts to do the lifting, shorten the hinge and think "hips back," not "bend down."
   Back: brace your core, keep a neutral spine, and stop if pain is sharp or runs down the leg. A little next-day stiffness is fine; sharper right-sided pain the next morning means cut the range or skip that day's hinges.
3. Warmup: standing side bend — 2 holds of 20–30 seconds. Stand tall, reach the left arm up and over to the left until there is a mild stretch along the right waist. It should feel like a stretch, not a burn.
4. Band seated calf raise — band looped around ball of foot; point toes into it. (seated calf raise)
5. Band leg extension — anchor low behind chair, band around ankle; straighten the knee. (leg extension)
6. Band front squat — stand on band, handles at shoulders; sit straight down, chest up. (hack squat)
7. Band hip thrust / glute bridge — band across hips, held down by hands or anchored; drive hips up, pause 2 seconds at the top, and squeeze the glutes hard as if pinching a coin. Ribs down; do not arch the low back. (hip thrust)
   Back: lift with the glutes, pause 2 seconds and squeeze hard at the top (as if pinching a coin), ribs down, do not arch the low back.
8. Band single-arm tricep pushdown — anchor high; elbow pinned at side, push to straight arm. (single-arm pushdown)
9. Band biceps curl — stand on band; elbows at sides, curl up. (DB curl)

   Back: brace your core, keep a neutral spine, and stop if pain is sharp or runs down the leg. A little next-day stiffness is fine; sharper right-sided pain the next morning means cut the range or skip that day's hinges.
3. Warmup: standing side bend — 2 holds of 20–30 seconds. Stand tall, reach the left arm up and over to the left until there is a mild stretch along the right waist. It should feel like a stretch, not a burn.
4. Band bent-over row — stand on band, flat back; hinge only as far as comfortable, row to belly. (barbell row)
   Back: brace, neutral spine, stop if pain is sharp or runs down the leg.
5. Band overhead press — stand on band; press handles straight overhead. (shoulder press)
6. Band single-arm row — anchor mid-height or stand on band; elbow to hip. (DB row)
7. Band chest press — anchor mid-height behind you, standing; press straight forward. (DB bench)
8. Band upright row — stand on band; elbows to shoulder height. Stop if shoulders pinch. (upright row)
9. Band deadlift — stand on band; hips back, neutral spine, stand tall. Go light while back is sore. (rack pull)
   Back: brace, neutral spine, stop if pain is sharp or runs down the leg.

5. Band close-grip chest press — anchor behind; hands close, elbows tucked. (close-grip bench)
6. Band drag curl — stand on band; drag hands up your body, elbows back. (drag curl)
7. Band overhead tricep extension — stand on band or anchor low, face away; extend overhead. (overhead cable extension)
8. Band single-arm concentration curl — seated, stand on band, elbow braced on inner thigh. (single-arm preacher curl)
9. Band split squat — front foot on band; rear foot elevated if comfortable; drop straight down. (Bulgarian split squat)
10. Band Romanian deadlift — stand on band, soft knees; push hips back, neutral spine. Light while back is sore. (RDL)
   Back: brace, neutral spine, stop if pain is sharp or runs down the leg.
